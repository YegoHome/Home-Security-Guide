Your Home Security Guide

Five short walks through your digital home

Think of your computer as your home. Programs open doors so other devices can reach them. Some doors you need. Some you don't.

Over five parts, you will look around, lock the risky doors, check any guest rooms, seal the conversations that still come through, and set up a regular patrol.


Steps last checked against official documentation: November 2026

The Five Parts

Take them in order. Each part builds on the one before it.

  1. 1The Security WalkLook only. Find which doors are open, who opened them, and which side of the house they face.
  2. 2Managing the LocksChoose your house type, then lock the risky doors with Windows Defender Firewall.
  3. 3The Guest RoomsFor virtual machines: lock each guest room and remove hallways (port forwarding) you no longer use.
  4. 4Sealed EnvelopesMake sure the doors you keep open use encryption (TLS), and check Remote Desktop and file sharing.
  5. 5The Regular PatrolA read-only logbook script, a schedule, safe temporary test doors, and a repeatable checklist.

Before You Start

What you need

  • A Windows 11 computer that you own or are allowed to change
  • About 15–30 minutes per part
  • An administrator account for Parts 2–5 (Windows will ask for permission)

Your edition of Windows matters

Feature in this guideWindows 11 HomeWindows 11 Pro / Enterprise
Windows Defender Firewall (Parts 1, 2, 5)YesYes
Hyper-V guest rooms (Part 3)NoYes
VirtualBox guest rooms (Part 3)Yes (separate download)Yes (separate download)
Accepting Remote Desktop visitors (Part 4)NoYes

Microsoft confirms that Hyper-V can't be installed on Windows 11 Home, and that Remote Desktop hosting needs a Pro edition of Windows.

To see your edition: press Windows + R, type winver, press Enter.

Three promises that hold in every part: we look before we change anything, every change can be undone, and each step tells you how to check it worked.
